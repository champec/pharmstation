## 1. Purpose

This document formally appoints the Clinical Safety Officer (CSO) for Pharmstation in accordance with **NHS Digital Standard DCB0129: Clinical Risk Management — its Application in the Manufacture of Health IT Systems** (current published edition).

The CSO is the named individual accountable to Pharmstation's senior management for the clinical safety of the Pharmstation health IT system across its full lifecycle (specification, design, build, release, deployment, modification, decommissioning).

### 3.1 Eligibility under DCB0129 §3.1

DCB0129 requires the CSO to be:

1. **A suitably qualified and experienced clinician** — Met. The appointee is a registered pharmacist on the GPhC register (2226350), actively practising in NHS community and primary-care settings.
2. **Holding a current registration with an appropriate professional body** — Met. GPhC registration is current and unrestricted.
3. **Trained in risk management and management of clinical risk in health IT** — Met. The appointee has completed self-directed study of DCB0129, DCB0160, ISO 14971 (medical device risk management), and the NHS Digital Clinical Safety Officer training materials. Formal NHS England CSO training will be undertaken if required by NHS England's Clinical Safety team during API assurance.

A CV evidencing clinical experience and training is held on file and will be supplied to NHS England on request as part of API assurance.

## 4. Authority and Responsibilities

The CSO is granted, by Pharmstation senior management, the authority and resources required to discharge the following responsibilities under DCB0129:

1. **Establish and maintain** the Clinical Risk Management System (CRMS) and the Clinical Risk Management Plan (CRMP) for Pharmstation (`PS-CRMP-001`).
2. **Identify, analyse, evaluate and control** clinical hazards arising from the use, mis-use, malfunction or absence of the Pharmstation health IT system, recorded in the Hazard Log (`PS-HAZLOG-001`).
3. **Author, sign off and maintain** the Clinical Safety Case Report (CSCR) (`PS-CSCR-001`) for each release of Pharmstation, and any subsequent material change.
4. **Approve or reject** product releases on clinical-safety grounds. The CSO has the authority to **block a release** if residual clinical risk is unacceptable.
5. **Ensure post-market surveillance** — review of incidents, adverse events, user feedback and field-safety information, with hazard log updates as required.
6. **Liaise with NHS England**, deploying organisations (pharmacies), and where relevant, the MHRA, on clinical safety matters.
7. **Co-operate with deploying organisations' Clinical Safety Officers** (DCB0160 deployment side) by providing the CSCR, hazard log extract, and any safety-relevant information they require.
8. **Maintain competence** through continuing professional development relevant to clinical risk management in health IT.

---

## 5. Resourcing

As a sole-trader manufacturer, the appointee is also the senior manager. This concentration of roles is **declared as a known organisational risk** in the Clinical Risk Management Plan. Mitigations:

- Independent peer review of the CSCR is sought from a second registered clinician for each major release.
- All clinical safety decisions are documented in writing in the hazard log with rationale, so they are auditable.
- Pharmstation will appoint a deputy CSO (registered clinician, independent of development) before the system is deployed to live patient care.

## 6. Term

This appointment is effective **30 April 2026** and remains in force until:

- The CSO resigns the role (with at least 30 days' written notice and an appointed successor), or
- Pharmstation senior management revokes the appointment in writing, or
- The CSO ceases to be eligible (e.g. loss of GPhC registration), in which case the appointment lapses immediately and a successor must be appointed before any further release of Pharmstation.

A review of this appointment is scheduled for **30 April 2027** or sooner if material change occurs.
