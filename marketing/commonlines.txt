GENERAL OPENERS

If you had one system that managed almost everything except your bad credit, would you use it?

Pharmacy has an app for everything. Unfortunately, it is usually a different app, a different password and a different invoice.

Pharmstation is what happens when a pharmacist gets tired of complaining about pharmacy software and accidentally learns how to build it.

I am not here to sell you another login. I am here to help you retire a few.

Pharmstation will not make the wholesaler deliver your owing, but it can make nearly everything after that less painful.

You already run a pharmacy, a clinic, a delivery service, a call centre and, on some days, a small crisis unit. Your software should at least try to keep up.

I built Pharmstation because "there must be a better way" was becoming my most repeated sentence at work.

I know every software company says it saves time. I would rather show you before the wholesaler's hold music starts again.

The aim is simple: less time doing pharmacy admin and more time doing actual pharmacy.


COMMUNICATION AND STOCK

They say good communication is the bridge to salvation. I am still wondering why pharmacies have been left on separate islands.

A patient needs one box, six pharmacies may have it, and somehow our national search technology is still "start at the top of Google and ring everybody."

We can track a takeaway to the driver's front door, but finding a box of medicine nearby still requires detective work and twenty phone calls.

The most expensive piece of pharmacy equipment is sometimes the phone, especially when six people use it to ask the same stock question.

Doctors should not have to spend their afternoon speed-dating local pharmacies: "Hello, do you have this? No? Lovely, next."

One secure broadcast can ask every nearby open pharmacy. It is the difference between making one announcement and going on a telephone tour of the high street.

Pharmacies are very good at helping each other. The problem is that our current network is usually one locum's contacts list.

We already know where every pharmacy is and when it should be open. Pharmstation adds the useful part: letting them speak to each other.

When a local pharmacy messages you, you know it is probably important. When an unknown number rings, it could be stock availability or somebody asking whether you sell stamps.

The NHS talks a lot about interoperability. Pharmstation starts with a radical first step: allowing two pharmacies to talk without playing phone tennis.

Even large chains struggle to see live stock across branches. Apparently the technology exists for satellites, but not for amoxicillin suspension.

A medicine sitting on one pharmacy's shelf should not be invisible to a patient standing two streets away.


ONE SYSTEM, FEWER LOGINS

I do not mind learning new things. I just object to learning twelve systems before I can complete one working day.

My password manager now has more pharmacy systems than I have friends. That felt like a sign.

RP notices here, CD register there, deliveries somewhere else and the SOP in a folder nobody has opened since 2019. Pharmstation brings the family back together.

Most pharmacy software solves one headache and gives you a fresh login as a side effect.

The dream is not more technology. The dream is technology you do not have to think about every five minutes.

Pharmstation is modular. Keep the systems you love, replace the ones you merely tolerate and pretend not to know the ones you hate.

Joining Pharmstation does not mean breaking up with every supplier on day one. Think of it as keeping your options open, professionally.

You can start free, use what is useful and add modules when they earn their place. No dramatic software wedding ceremony required.

Vendor lock-in is like being trapped in a bad relationship, except the breakup fee comes as a PDF invoice.
